02The maturity model

Six levels, from manual to AI-native.

Maturity is not a feeling; it is a level. The ladder makes the transition legible — every capability, every industry, every team can be placed on it, and every quarter is judged by how far it moves up. EY today sits around L1–L2. AI-native is L5.

How to read the ladder

The jump that matters is L2 → L3: the moment governance stops being a review you pass and becomes code that runs — the Gate. Everything above L3 compounds; everything at or below it is still bolted on. The whole roadmap is engineered to cross that line in banking first, then carry it everywhere.

04How we measure success

A scorecard a managing partner would accept.

AI-native has to be provable in numbers a CFO and a managing partner trust — not vanity metrics. Twelve indicators across four areas, each with a direction of good and a today / +12-month / +3-year trajectory. The four groups track the climb up the ladder: Governance & Trust proves the move to L3, Capability & Leverage and Economics prove L4, and the full mix — outcomes, diamond workforce, compounding reuse — proves L5. These are Keystone-proposed planning targets, not EY commitments.

04·BWhat we must also carry

The ladder is not the whole job.

A maturity model measures capability; it does not, on its own, fund the work, clear independence, secure the data, or onboard a bank. These are the cross-cutting workstreams the roadmap has to run alongside the pillars — the ones that actually decide whether the plan survives contact with a regulated firm. They are named here so they are not discovered late.

The honest caveat

Two of these — the independence firewall and client procurement — move slower than any other line on the plan. They are the reason the honest read of the dates re-dates the later phases. Treat them as Pillar Zero, run ahead of Phase 0.
